## At a glance

The Gyeongbokgung night opening lets you see the palace under lighting, in a completely different mood from a daytime visit, and tickets are famously competitive every year. The 2026 second-half run is from September 2 to October 2, and it's worth knowing about the hanbok free-entry program as a way to save on admission.

## Free entry - the hanbok requirement

If you wear hanbok that meets the requirements, you can enter on-site for free without a reservation. However, this only applies if you're wearing a full set &mdash; a jeogori top with either a skirt or pants. Wearing only a top or only a bottom, wearing just a durumagi coat, or pairing a jeogori with jeans does not qualify, so it's safer to check your outfit before you go. Without hanbok, you'll use the regular paid, pre-booked entry.

## How to book, and getting there

Gyeongbokgung night viewing tickets are released on a first-come, first-served basis through the official booking agent designated by the Korea Heritage Service. The release time, closing time, and cancellation deadline are set for each period, so it's worth checking the official notice again before you go. Popular time slots often sell out quickly right after release, so it helps to decide on your preferred date and group size in advance and be ready when booking opens.

Gyeongbokgung is an easy walk from Gyeongbokgung Station on Line 3 or Gwanghwamun Station on Line 5, so public transit works well. Night viewing ends later than daytime hours, so it's worth checking your route home or the last public transit times in advance.

## Before you go

- **Regular closing days and hours**: these can vary by period, so check before booking
- **Booking release time**: tickets are first-come, first-served and popular slots sell out fast
- **Hanbok requirements**: free entry only applies to a jeogori worn with a matching skirt or pants
- **Cancellation deadline**: check the cutoff for canceling your booking
- **Getting home**: check the night viewing's end time against your last available transit

## Trip planning notes

If you're combining the night viewing with a walk through Gwanghwamun, Insadong, or Bukchon, it's worth building in extra time for getting around. Some travelers pair an early dinner near Hoehyeon Station with a stop by [Namdaemun Market's galchi alley](/travel/namdaemun-galchi-alley-food-hours/) before the night viewing &mdash; just plan for the travel time and eat a bit earlier than usual. Since this itinerary involves a lot of walking, staying near [Jongno or Gwanghwamun](/stays/seoul-stay-area/) can also help shorten your routes. Booking details, admission fees, and the free-entry conditions can change based on notices from the Korea Heritage Service and the booking agent, so check the latest official information before you go.
